Your watch, over HTTP

Apple ships no web API for Health, and Garmin's is closed to individuals. This is one for both: any device can add your workouts, and you can read them from anywhere. The server stores only ciphertext and cannot read a single record.

Use it yourself, in four steps

  1. Make an account on HuskyHealth. Your browser turns your password into two keys: one unlocks your vault and never leaves your device, the other logs you in. It also makes a keypair; the server keeps only the public half.
  2. Connect your watch. Log in on the iPhone app: it sends your last 60 days and every new workout, from an Apple Watch or a Garmin synced to Apple Health. Or make a device token on your account and send records from any script.
  3. Records are sealed on arrival. The server checks each record, encrypts it to your public key and keeps only ciphertext, for at most 30 days or until you collect it. Nobody at the server can read it.
  4. Read them back with your password: on the website, with node scripts/huskyhealth.mjs history --user <you> (your whole history) or records (new records not yet picked up), or by speaking to it (“how was my week?”) on the today page. Each answer shows the request behind it.

Base URL https://veds-pi5.polecat-nominal.ts.net:10000. A record holds workouts (sport, start and end as seconds before sending, heart rate), resting heart rate, HRV, VO2 max, minutes asleep per night and Watch effort. Names, places, GPS and device names are rejected.

Limits

whatlimit
log-in attempts10 per account per 15 minutes
records120 per device token per hour, 300 per account
reads and token changes120 per session per minute
device tokenswrite-only; expire after 1–365 days (default 90); 10 per account
bodiesJSON only; records up to 128 KB

Over a limit you get 429 with Retry-After. Errors are always { "error", "status" }; every response carries API-Version: 1.1. Version 1.1 added vo2 and sleep to records; changes inside /v1/ only ever add fields.

Try it

Make a device token on your account, then send a run:

curl -X POST https://veds-pi5.polecat-nominal.ts.net:10000/v1/me/records \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $HH_TOKEN" -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{"v":1,"workouts":[{"sport":"running","start_s":3600,"end_s":600,
       "hr":[{"t_s":3500,"v":142},{"t_s":3490,"v":145}]}],
       "effort":[{"t_s":600,"v":6}]}'

Read it back, decrypted on your machine:

node scripts/huskyhealth.mjs records --user <you>

Or your whole saved history, every workout and day, e.g. your last squash session:

node scripts/huskyhealth.mjs history --user <you> --sport squash --last 1

Or with curl, to see exactly what the Pi holds. A session token lets curl fetch your history; it arrives as ciphertext, and only your password opens it, on your machine:

export HH_SESSION=$(node scripts/huskyhealth.mjs session --user <you>)
curl https://veds-pi5.polecat-nominal.ts.net:10000/v1/account/vault \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $HH_SESSION" -o vault.json
node scripts/huskyhealth.mjs open vault.json --user <you> --sport squash --last 1
